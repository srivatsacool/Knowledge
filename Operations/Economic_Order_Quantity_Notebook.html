Conceptual Foundations & Trade-Off Mechanics

💡 Key Learning Objective: Master the fundamental trade-off between inventory holding costs and ordering costs, and understand the governing assumptions of the Ford W. Harris (1913) classical model.
⏱ Section Target: 10 min 10:00

The Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) is the foundational model of deterministic inventory management, originally derived by Ford Whitman Harris in 1913 and later popularized by R. H. Wilson. It calculates the optimal order size $Q^*$ that minimizes total annual inventory management costs.

💡 The Core Trade-Off: Ordering costs per year decrease as order size increases (fewer orders placed), whereas holding costs per year increase linearly with order size (more average inventory sitting in warehouses). The optimal point $Q^*$ occurs at their exact intersection.

Governing Axiomatic Assumptions

  • Deterministic Demand ($D$): Annual demand is constant, known, and evenly distributed over time.
  • Instantaneous Replenishment: The entire batch $Q$ arrives instantaneously in a single delivery (lead time $L$ is constant and known).
  • Fixed Unit Cost ($C$): Purchase price per unit is constant; no quantity discounts are offered in the basic model.
  • Fixed Setup/Ordering Cost ($S$): Every order incurs a fixed administrative and freight cost, regardless of the quantity ordered.
  • Constant Carrying Cost ($H$): Cost to hold one unit for one year ($H = C \times i$) is constant, including capital costs, insurance, taxes, obsolescence, and storage.
  • Zero Stockouts Allowed: Demand is always fulfilled immediately; shortages and backorders are forbidden.

Calculus Derivation & Cost Curve Architecture

💡 Key Learning Objective: Derive the EOQ formula from first principles using single-variable differential calculus and prove global convexity via second-order conditions.
⏱ Section Target: 15 min 15:00

Total Annual Inventory Cost ($TC$) is expressed as the sum of annual purchase cost, annual ordering cost, and annual holding cost:

Total Annual Cost Equation

$TC(Q) = D \cdot C + \left(\frac{D}{Q}\right)S + \left(\frac{Q}{2}\right)H$

  • Annual Purchase Cost: $D \cdot C$ (independent of $Q$)
  • Annual Ordering Cost: $\frac{D}{Q} \cdot S$ (number of orders per year multiplied by fixed cost per order $S$)
  • Annual Carrying Cost: $\frac{Q}{2} \cdot H$ (average inventory level $\bar{I} = Q/2$ multiplied by unit holding cost $H$)

First-Order Condition (Minimization Proof)

Differentiating total variable cost $TVC(Q) = \frac{DS}{Q} + \frac{QH}{2}$ with respect to $Q$ and setting the derivative equal to zero:

$\frac{d(TVC)}{dQ} = -\frac{D \cdot S}{Q^2} + \frac{H}{2} = 0$

Equating the marginal ordering cost to marginal carrying cost:

$\frac{D \cdot S}{Q^2} = \frac{H}{2} \implies Q^2 = \frac{2DS}{H} \implies Q^* = \sqrt{\frac{2DS}{H}}$

Proof of Global Minimum: Taking the second derivative:$\frac{d^2(TVC)}{dQ^2} = \frac{2DS}{Q^3} > 0 \quad \forall \, Q, D, S > 0$Since the second derivative is strictly positive for all physical parameter values, the curve is strictly convex and $Q^*$ is the unique global minimum.

Applied Numerical Master Case & Reorder Point

💡 Key Learning Objective: Solve an end-to-end industrial scenario, compute the Reorder Point (ROP), and evaluate square-root sensitivity to demand and cost shocks.
⏱ Section Target: 20 min 20:00

A high-tech industrial assembly plant consumes specialized precision micro-valves with the following parameters:

Scenario Input Parameters

  • Annual Demand ($D$): 12,000 units/year
  • Order / Setup Cost ($S$): $60.00 per order
  • Unit Holding Cost ($H$): $4.00 per unit/year (Inventory carrying rate $i = 20\%$ on unit cost $C = \$20.00$)
  • Operating Schedule: 300 working days/year
  • Supplier Lead Time ($L$): 5 working days

Step-by-Step Numerical Solution

  1. Optimal Batch Size ($Q^*$):$Q^* = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 12,000 \times 60}{4}} = \sqrt{360,000} = 600 \text{ units}$
  2. Annual Order Frequency ($N$):$N = \frac{D}{Q^*} = \frac{12,000}{600} = 20 \text{ orders per year}$
  3. Order Cycle Time ($T$):$T = \frac{300 \text{ days}}{20} = 15 \text{ working days between orders}$
  4. Total Annual Variable Cost ($TVC^*$):$TVC^* = \left(\frac{12,000}{600} \times 60\right) + \left(\frac{600}{2} \times 4\right) = \$1,200 + \$1,200 = \$2,400.00$Notice: At optimality, annual ordering cost exactly equals annual holding cost ($1,200 = $1,200).
  5. Reorder Point ($ROP$):$\text{Daily Demand } d = \frac{12,000}{300} = 40 \text{ units/day}$$ROP = d \times L = 40 \times 5 = 200 \text{ units}$Order trigger: Place a replenishment order of 600 units the instant warehouse stock drops to 200 units.
Robustness & Sensitivity Analysis: The square-root property creates an exceptionally forgiving cost curve. If the plant orders $Q = 750$ units (a $+25\%$ deviation above optimal), the total variable cost increases from $\$2,400$ to only $\$2,460$ (a mere $+2.5\%$ cost penalty).

Exam Defense & Model Question Bank

💡 Key Learning Objective: Prepare for high-level technical exams and interview defense regarding model limitations, POQ extensions, and quantity discount pricing.
⏱ Section Target: 15 min 15:00

Question 1: What happens to EOQ when demand quadruples ($4D$)?

Model Answer: Because demand enters under the square root sign, $Q^*_{\text{new}} = \sqrt{\frac{2(4D)S}{H}} = \sqrt{4} \times \sqrt{\frac{2DS}{H}} = 2 \times Q^*$. Order quantity only doubles, while order frequency ($N = D/Q$) also doubles. Inventory does not scale linearly with business volume—a classic demonstration of economies of scale in supply chain operations.

Question 2: How does the Economic Production Quantity (EPQ / POQ) modify Classical EOQ?

Model Answer: Classical EOQ assumes infinite replenishment rate ($p = \infty$). When units are manufactured in-house at a daily rate $p$ while being depleted at rate $d$ ($p > d$), inventory accumulates at rate $(p - d)$. The maximum inventory is $Q(1 - d/p)$, reducing holding costs and expanding optimal batch size to:$Q^*_{\text{EPQ}} = \sqrt{\frac{2DS}{H(1 - d/p)}}$

Question 3: How should quantity discounts be analyzed?

Model Answer: Quantity discount analysis requires evaluating total cost including purchase price ($TC = DC + \frac{DS}{Q} + \frac{QH}{2}$). The procedure: (1) Compute EOQ for the lowest price bracket; (2) If feasible, that is the optimal size; (3) If not feasible, compute total cost at each price breakpoint price drop and select the quantity with minimum total cost.
