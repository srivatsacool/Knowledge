Module 1 · TLP topic 1.0

Why ERP? Relevance & Concept

COREPYQCASE STUDYLINKED TO 3 OF 3 PAPERS
01

Why this matters. Every one of the three supplied papers opens with a compulsory ERP-strategy case. This module gives you the argument that case answers are built on: ERP is a business philosophy first and software second.

Learning objectives

  • State the four “relevance” drivers from the faculty slides
  • Reproduce the three concept statements of ERP
  • Argue why ERP is a business strategy, not merely an IT project

Three statements of what ERP is

SOURCE The faculty “Concepts” slide gives three statements:

  1. A planning methodology or philosophy based on the seamless integration of all the business processes of an enterprise.
  2. A set of software covering finance, logistics, sales, materials, manufacturing, distribution etc., so tightly integrated that any business activity recorded at one place is immediately reflected in all other places.
  3. The finest expression of the inseparability of Info-tech and business — an enterprise-wide system with enabling technology and an effective managerial tool for integrating all levels and improving reportability.

Source: Faculty Reading Material 01, “Concepts” slide.

Faculty terminology — Relevance & Agenda

SOURCE Relevance slide: Changing Business Scenario · Role of Information in Decision Making · Need to act as “One System” · Performance V/S Perceptions.

SOURCE Agenda slide: Introduction · Conceptual Origination · Evolution · Structure & Architecture · Global Best Practices · Myths.

Source: Faculty Reading Material 01, slides 2–3.

Detailed explanation

EXPLANATION Read the three statements as three lenses: statement 1 = management lens (philosophy), statement 2 = system lens (software), statement 3 = fusion lens (IT and business cannot be separated). An exam answer that only uses lens 2 sounds like an IT answer — that is precisely the trap in all three Q1 cases.

EXPLANATION “Need to act as One System”: departments that plan on their own data make locally sensible but globally inconsistent decisions. “Performance v/s Perceptions” is listed as a heading only; its detailed meaning is not in the supplied slides — treat as source wording unclear and confirm from class notes.

Visual: from separate systems to one system

Example

ILLUSTRATIVE Illustrative only: a pharmaceutical company books one customer order. In an integrated system the same entry updates available stock, triggers production/purchase planning and creates the receivable — nobody re-types it.

Application — the “business vs IT” argument

  • EXPLANATION Business objective first (growth, customer centricity), technology second.
  • EXPLANATION Process integration changes how people work and decide, not just where data sits.
  • SOURCE Statement 3: IT and business are inseparable, so the “technology intervention” framing alone is incomplete.

Exam angle

PYQ signal: ERP-as-business-strategy appears in the compulsory Q1 of 3 of 3 supplied papers (2023, 2024, 2025). Open every strategy answer with statement 1 + 3.

Common confusion

“ERP = software.” The faculty myths list explicitly contains “ERP is software system” and “ERP is computerization project of the company” as myths.

Quick check

Which concept statement stresses the inseparability of Info-tech and business?

EXPLANATION The third statement — “the finest expression of the inseparability of Info-tech and business”.

  • 2025 Q1 (20 marks) — Ms. Vijaya Loki, newly appointed CDO of LPU Ltd, a renowned Chemical company in India, has an immediate cha…

PYQ: ERP Business Applications — supplied papers (2023, 2024, 2025).

  • ERP = philosophy + integrated software + inseparability of IT & business.
  • Activity recorded once, reflected everywhere.
  • Cases reward the business-strategy framing.

Learning objectives

Faculty architecture slide

SOURCE “Client-Server (2/3/n Tier)”. Components drawn: Repository, GUI Driver, Logic Server, DB Driver, RDBMS, all sitting on Operating System (Unix, NT).

Source: Faculty Reading Material 01, slide 7.

Visual: architecture (redrawn)

Detailed explanation

EXPLANATION GUI driver = what the user sees (presentation). Logic server = business rules/processing. DB driver + RDBMS = storing and retrieving the single shared data. Repository = the stored definitions/metadata the system runs on. Operating system hosts all of it.

EXPLANATION 2-tier = client talks directly to the database server; 3-tier = a separate application/logic layer in the middle; n-tier = further layers (e.g., web, integration). More tiers → easier scaling and maintenance.

SOURCE The TLP lists “Importance of RDBMS” and “Introduction to Analytics” under topic 7.0; the supplied slides give only the RDBMS box in the architecture. No analytics slide was supplied.

Example

ILLUSTRATIVE A user in a warehouse opens a screen (GUI) → the logic server checks stock rules → the DB driver fetches the item record from the RDBMS.

Exam angle

Pair this with M3 for the 10-mark “conceptual model and architectural aspects” answer; a clean diagram + 3 lines of explanation gains marks quickly.

Common confusion

“Tier” counts layers of the client-server design — not the number of modules.

Quick check

EXPLANATION Operating System (Unix, NT).

Learning objectives

  • Place MTS / ATO-CTO / MTO / ETO on the CODP diagram

Best practice & core themes

SOURCE Best Practice = a practice that uses the 5 pillars to generate Customer Focus · Minimal Waste of Resources · Value Creation.

SOURCE Core themes: CODP · Demand Management to SNOP · Organizational Structure · ABC.

Source: Faculty Reading Material 01, slide 10.

Visual: CODP (redrawn)

CODP, ATO and MTO

SOURCE Board: stages RM, Components, SFG, FG; scenarios MTS, ATO/CTO, MTO, ETO, each with a circled point on its row.

EXPLANATION CODP = Customer Order Decoupling Point: the stage up to which you plan to forecast and after which the customer order pulls. Further to the right (near FG) = more stock-driven (MTS); further left = more customer-specific (ETO).

EXPLANATION Value of ERP in ATO/MTO (2025 Q2): keeps components/semi-finished goods planned against forecast, then triggers final assembly/manufacture only on the order; gives realistic promise dates from shared stock + capacity; cuts finished-goods inventory; one plan across sales, planning, purchasing.

SNOP & ABC

SOURCE Slide wording: “Demand Management to SNOP”. EXPLANATION SNOP is generally expanded as Sales & Operations Planning — the monthly cross-functional process that balances demand (sales plan) against supply (capacity, materials, inventory) and produces one agreed plan that feeds the master schedule. The expansion is not written on the supplied slide.

SOURCE Slide wording: “ABC”. Source wording unclear — the abbreviation is not expanded. EXPLANATION In an ERP/inventory context it is usually ABC analysis: rank items by annual value so that few A items (high value) get tight control, many C items (low value) get simple control. “ABC” also abbreviates Activity-Based Costing; confirm with Sir.

SOURCE “Organizational Structure” is listed as a core theme with no detail; EXPLANATION read together with pillar 1 (process-based flat organisation).

Example

ILLUSTRATIVE Automobile (ATO): engines/gearboxes stocked to forecast; the car is finished with the colour/trim/options the dealer ordered. ILLUSTRATIVE ABC: 10% of SKUs carry most of the value — count them monthly; low-value fasteners — reorder bins.

Application — answer structure

  1. Define best practice (5 pillars → customer focus, minimal waste, value creation).
  2. Take each core theme: CODP → SNOP → organisation → ABC.
  3. One example each; tie back to the case company.

Exam angle

Best-practices family (best practices, ATO/MTO, SNOP, ABC): 2024 Q4, 2025 Q2, 2025 Q4 — appears in 2 of 3 supplied papers.

Common confusion

CODP is a point in the supply chain, not a department. ATO and MTO differ in where the CODP is.

Quick check

In the CODP board, which scenario’s circle sits at FG (nearest the customer)?

EXPLANATION MTS (make-to-stock).

Module 6 · TLP topic 3.0

Plossl’s Theory of Manufacturing

CORECONCEPTUALREVISION
06

Why this matters. Twelve crisp principles on two faculty slides — ideal for definitions, one-liners and MCQs, though it has not yet been asked directly in the supplied papers.

Learning objectives

  • Recall the twelve principles
  • Group them into four themes
  • Use them to justify ERP-based planning

The twelve principles (faculty wording)

  1. Production problems must and can be eliminated; they cannot be covered up successfully with cushions of inventory and time.
  2. Inventory is more of a liability than an asset, having real value only when it is flowing through operations or used to support them.
  3. Tolerating some downtime while striving to eliminate their causes is better than preventing idle time by manufacturing items not required immediately.
  4. More frequent and precise re-planning (daily rather than weekly) does not make it more accurate.
  5. Re-planning is admitting failure; it’s not a substitute for sound execution.
  6. Plans impossible to execute are worse than useless.
  7. Lead times cannot only be monitored and adjusted but can also be controlled.
  8. Reducing setup time is worth the effort.
  9. Planning defines resources needed to make what is planned; execution applies available resources to make what customers want now.
  10. Only resources requiring long periods for actions should be planned ahead; detailed plans should cover only very short horizons.
  11. There is one manufacturing planning and control system framework common to all types of manufacturing.
  12. All employees need continuous education.

Source: Faculty Reading Material 01, slides 12–13.

Four themes (study grouping)

ThemePrinciplesOne-line takeaway
Inventory & problems1, 2, 3Fix causes; do not hide them in stock or idle-time avoidance
Planning discipline4, 5, 6, 9, 10Plan only what can be executed; keep detail short-horizon
Lead time & set-up7, 8Both are controllable, not fixed
Framework & people11, 12One MP&C framework; continuous education

EXPLANATION The grouping is a memory aid created for this notebook, not Sir’s categorisation.

Application

EXPLANATION Link to ERP: shared real-time data supports principle 7 (lead times controlled), principle 9 (plan vs execute) and principle 11 (one framework for every manufacturing type).

Exam angle

Not asked directly in the 3 supplied papers. Faculty slides emphasise it (two full slides); treat as MCQ / short-note material.

Common confusion

Principle 4 does not say “never re-plan”; it says higher frequency/precision does not equal higher accuracy.

Quick check

Plossl: inventory is more of a ______ than an asset.

EXPLANATION Liability — real value only when flowing through operations or supporting them.

Revision summary

  • 12 principles → 4 themes.
  • Keywords: liability · re-planning = failure · lead times controllable · set-up reduction · one framework · continuous education.

Faculty module list

SOURCE Manufacturing (Capacity Planning, Item Control) · Distribution · Financial. TLP topic 4.0: “Functional Modules of ERP: Manufacturing, Distribution, Financials etc.”

Source: Faculty Reading Material 02 slide 3; TLP.

Visual: module groups

Framework: ERP business process view (redrawn)

Reading the process view

SOURCE Nodes on the figure include Depots, Warehouses, Transportation Planning & Control, DRP, Customers, Sales Order, Production Planning, MPS, MRP, BOM, EDM, Routing, Service Requirements, Purchase Order, Shop Floor, Inventory Records, Supplier, Fixed Assets, Costing, General Ledger, Accounts Receivable/Payable, Cash Management, Bank, Budgets, Financial Statements.

EXPLANATION Story to tell: sales order + distribution requirements + production planning → MPS → MRP (using BOM, routing, inventory records) → purchase orders and shop-floor orders → inventory → costing and General Ledger → financial statements.

Item control, phantom item, MPS, capacity planning

The supplied slides list these terms but do not define them. Definitions below are EXPLANATION general model knowledge — verify against your class notes.

  • EXPLANATION Item control: managing the item master and its data (identity, BOM linkage, lead times, lot-size rules, stock status, ABC class) so that planning and inventory transactions are accurate. Application: control stock by class, trigger replenishment, avoid obsolete/excess stock.
  • EXPLANATION Phantom item: a sub-assembly that exists in the BOM for structure/costing but is not stocked — MRP explodes straight through it to its components.
  • EXPLANATION MPS (Master Production Schedule): the committed plan of what end items to build, how many, when — the link between demand and MRP.
  • EXPLANATION Capacity planning: checking that the plan fits available work-centre capacity, at master-schedule level (rough-cut) and detailed level.

Example — automobile MPS (2023 Q4)

ILLUSTRATIVE Illustrative: dealer orders + sales forecast for model variants → SNOP-approved volumes → MPS by week/plant → MRP explodes engines, trims and bought-outs → purchase and shop orders → inventory and cost postings. Phantom item: a pre-assembled seat sub-kit that is never stocked.

Exam angle

Seen in 2 of 3 papers: 2023 Q2 (functional modules + item control), 2023 Q4 (automobile MPS), 2024 Q2 (manufacturing module + phantom item). 10 marks each, BL3, CO2.

Common confusion

MPS (end items) vs MRP (components). Phantom item is a BOM structure device, not a stocked item.

Quick check

Which two items are listed under the Manufacturing module on the faculty slide?

EXPLANATION Capacity Planning and Item Control.

  • 2024 Q2 (10 marks) — Explain briefly manufacturing module of ERP. What is Phantom Item?

  • Flow: Sales order → MPS → MRP → PO/Shop floor → Inventory → GL → Financial statements.
  • Phantom item = non-stocked BOM sub-assembly.

Visual: the paradox

Study framework — what first? Why?

  1. EXPLANATION BPR = rethinking and redesigning processes for step-change gains (cost, quality, speed, service).
  2. EXPLANATION Case for BPR first: automating a bad process only makes it faster; a process vision tells you what to configure.
  3. EXPLANATION Case for ERP first: ERP embeds “best practice” processes; adopting them is itself reengineering, and a blank-sheet redesign can ignore what the software can do.
  4. EXPLANATION Defensible conclusion: do a light, vision-level BPR first (readiness audit, target processes), then let ERP best practices and implementation refine it — iterate. State a clear first step and justify it.
