What is Marketing Management?

Marketing Management is the art and science of choosing target markets and getting, keeping, and growing customers through creating, delivering, and communicating superior customer value. Unlike advertising or sales—which are tactical execution arms—strategic marketing begins before any product exists and continues throughout the entire customer lifecycle.

Peter Drucker’s Foundational Axiom: "Because the purpose of business is to create a customer, the business enterprise has two—and only two—basic functions: marketing and innovation. Marketing and innovation produce results; all the rest are costs."

Strategic marketing requires understanding the Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) framework: customers do not buy products or services; they "hire" them to make progress in specific circumstances.

SECTION 02 OF 10

Why Marketing Matters in an MBA

Marketing is the single primary business discipline that connects the external customer ecosystem directly to the internal revenue engine of the firm. Without marketing insight, engineering teams build products nobody wants, finance models unrealistic sales projections, and supply chains produce surplus inventory.

In modern data-driven enterprises, marketing is increasingly analytical and quantitative. MBA marketing equips leaders with:

  • Unit Economics Discipline: Understanding customer acquisition economics (CAC), retention dynamics, and Customer Lifetime Value (CLV).
  • Brand Equity Valuation: Quantifying intangible brand assets on enterprise balance sheets.
  • Pricing Power: Designing monetization architectures that capture maximum consumer surplus without eroding demand.
SECTION 03 OF 10

Core Concepts of Marketing Management

1. The 5Cs Situation Analysis

The foundational diagnostic scanner: Company (capabilities, brand), Customers (needs, segments), Competitors (positioning, moats), Collaborators (suppliers, distributors), and Context (PESTLE macro-environment).

2. The STP Process

Segmentation: Partitioning the market into distinct groups. Targeting: Selecting the most attractive segments. Positioning: Securing a distinct, credible mental shelf-space in the customer's mind.

3. The 4Ps Marketing Mix

The tactical execution levers: Product (features, quality, design), Price (skimming, penetration, dynamic), Place (distribution channels, direct vs. retail), and Promotion (integrated marketing communications).

4. Customer Acquisition vs. Retention

Acquiring a new customer costs 5 to 7 times more than retaining an existing one. A 5% increase in customer retention can increase profits by 25% to 95% depending on industry gross margins.

Key Terminology Every Manager Must Know

TermDefinitionStrategic Relevance
CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)Total sales and marketing expenditure divided by the number of new customers acquired.The critical benchmark against which customer lifetime value is measured. If CAC > LTV/3, the business model burns cash unsustainably.
Churn RatePercentage of customers or subscribers who cancel or fail to renew over a given period.The leaky bucket of subscription economics. Compound churn accelerates revenue decay and forces hyper-aggressive customer acquisition.
Net Promoter Score (NPS)% Promoters (ratings 9-10) minus % Detractors (ratings 0-6) on a 0-10 recommendation scale.A leading indicator of organic customer advocacy, viral coefficient, and structural brand loyalty.
Price Elasticity of DemandPercentage change in quantity demanded in response to a 1% change in price.Determines pricing power. Inelastic products (|e| < 1) allow price hikes to directly increase gross revenue.
Brand Equity (Keller's CBBE)The differential effect that brand knowledge has on consumer response to marketing actions.Allows firms to command price premiums, insulate against competitive promotions, and expand into adjacent categories.
SECTION 05 OF 10

Major Frameworks and Analytical Models

1. The Positioning Statement (HBS Format)

"For [Target Customer Segment] who [Statement of Need/Opportunity], [Product/Brand Name] is a [Product Category] that [Statement of Key Benefit/Compelling Reason to Buy]. Unlike [Primary Competitive Alternative], our product [Statement of Primary Differentiation]."

2. AARRR Pirate Metrics Funnel (McClure)

Modern digital and growth marketing framework: Acquisition (users discover product) → Activation (users experience "Aha!" moment) → Retention (users return repeatedly) → Revenue (users pay/transact) → Referral (users invite peers).

3. Bass Diffusion Model

Predicts how new innovations are adopted across a population over time through innovators (p coefficient) and imitators (q coefficient via word-of-mouth). Explains S-shaped technology adoption curves.

SECTION 06 OF 10

Important Formulas & Quantitative Measures

Customer Lifetime Value (CLV / LTV) - Infinite Horizon CLV = [Margin × Retention Rate] / [1 + Discount Rate - Retention Rate]
Where Margin is annual contribution margin per customer, Retention Rate is annual retention probability (1 - churn), and Discount Rate is the corporate cost of capital.
LTV to CAC Ratio & Payback Period LTV : CAC ≥ 3.0x  |  Payback Period = CAC / (Monthly ARPU × Gross Margin) ≤ 12 Months
Gold-standard health metrics for SaaS and subscription businesses. Ratios below 3x indicate inefficient marketing; ratios above 5x indicate under-investing in growth.
Price Elasticity of Demand (Ped) ε = (% Δ Quantity Demanded) / (% Δ Price) = (dQ/dP) × (P/Q)
When |ε| > 1, demand is elastic (price reductions increase total revenue); when |ε| < 1, demand is inelastic (price increases boost revenue).

Typical Strategic Business Decisions Involved

Pricing Strategy: Skimming vs. Penetration

Setting high initial prices to skim consumer surplus from early adopters before lowering prices (Apple iPhone), versus low initial penetration prices to capture network scale and lock in market share (Uber, Spotify).

Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) vs. Wholesale Retail

Evaluating channel conflict: DTC provides rich first-party data and 100% margin capture but requires heavy digital customer acquisition spend; retail provides immediate physical distribution but demands wholesale discounts.

Product Portfolio Extension & Cannibalization

Launching a "fighter brand" or lower-tier offering. Will the new lower-priced product expand total category market share or simply cannibalize existing high-margin sales?

Brand Architecture: House of Brands vs. Branded House

Choosing between an umbrella brand where all products carry the master name (Virgin, Google, FedEx) versus standalone independent product brands (Procter & Gamble: Tide, Pampers, Gillette).

SECTION 08 OF 10

Real-World Business Applications

Case 1: HubSpot’s Inbound Marketing & CLV Engineering

Instead of cold outreach, HubSpot pioneered educational content marketing (ebooks, certifications, free tools) to attract high-intent leads organically. By lowering CAC while expanding retention through modular product hubs, they engineered an industry-leading LTV:CAC profile.

Case 2: Coca-Cola vs. Pepsi: The Value of Brand Positioning

In blind taste tests (the Pepsi Challenge), consumers frequently preferred Pepsi's sweeter formula. However, when labels were revealed, brain fMRI scans showed enhanced activation in memory and emotion centers for Coca-Cola, proving that customer willingness to pay is driven by brand narrative, heritage, and emotional resonance rather than physical product attributes alone.

SECTION 09 OF 10

Cross-Disciplinary Connections to Other MBA Fields

  • Finance: Marketing spend is no longer treated as discretionary overhead; it is evaluated as an investment portfolio with expected Net Present Value (NPV) and return on marketing investment (ROMI).
  • Operations: Marketing demand forecasts drive Sales & Operations Planning (S&OP), production scheduling, and inventory safety stock levels.
  • Strategy: Strategic differentiation is manifested through brand identity and positioning. Without marketing validation, competitive strategies lack customer reality.
  • Analytics: Marketing is the primary driver of commercial analytics, including marketing mix modeling (MMM), multi-touch attribution, and algorithmic recommendation engines.
