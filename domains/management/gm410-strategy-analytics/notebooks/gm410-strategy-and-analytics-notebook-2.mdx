# 01 — Strategic Intent & Goal Architecture

Strategy is the art of creating superior, sustainable enterprise value through deliberate trade-offs, distinctive positioning, and tight organisational alignment.

<Callout type="note" title="Mintzberg's 5 Ps of Strategy">
  Henry Mintzberg established that strategy cannot be reduced to a single linear plan. Strategy operates across five simultaneous dimensions:
  1. **Plan**: Conscious, intended course of action.
  2. **Ploy**: Specific maneuver to outwit competitors.
  3. **Pattern**: Consistency in behavior over time (emergent strategy).
  4. **Position**: Locating the firm in its external competitive space.
  5. **Perspective**: Fundamental worldview and cultural identity of the enterprise.
</Callout>

### Deliberate vs. Emergent Strategy

* **Intended Strategy**: Planned strategy derived from formal analytical forecasting.
* **Deliberate Strategy**: Realised intentions where plans are faithfully executed without significant deviation.
* **Unrealised Strategy**: Planned initiatives discarded due to environmental shifts or execution bottlenecks.
* **Emergent Strategy**: Realised patterns of behavior that evolved in response to unexpected market realities (e.g., Honda's Super Cub entry into the US motorcycle market).
* **Realised Strategy**: The actual synthetic outcome of deliberate plans combined with emergent patterns.

# 02 — External Environmental Analysis

Macro-environmental scanning and industry structure determine long-term industry profitability and competitive attractiveness.

### Porter's Five Forces Framework

1. **Threat of New Entrants**: Determined by economies of scale, capital requirements, switching costs, and access to distribution channels.
2. **Bargaining Power of Suppliers**: High when inputs are concentrated, switching costs are elevated, or credible threats of forward integration exist.
3. **Bargaining Power of Buyers**: High when purchase volumes are large, products are undifferentiated, or switching costs are negligible.
4. **Threat of Substitute Products**: Evaluates relative price-to-performance tradeoffs and buyer propensity to substitute.
5. **Rivalry Among Existing Competitors**: Driven by industry growth rate, exit barriers, fixed costs, and competitor diversity.

# 03 — Internal Capabilities & Implementation

Sustainable competitive advantage arises from bundling valuable, rare, and inimitable internal resources and aligning them with strategy.

### VRIO Framework (Barney)

* **Value**: Does the resource allow the firm to exploit opportunities or neutralize environmental threats?
* **Rarity**: Is the resource controlled by a small number of competing firms?
* **Inimitability**: Do competitors face severe cost disadvantages in acquiring or developing the resource (historical path dependency, causal ambiguity, social complexity)?
* **Organization**: Is the firm structured, governed, and culturally organized to capture the full economic potential of the resource?

### McKinsey 7S Framework

* **Hard Elements**: Strategy, Structure, Systems (tangible, directly alterable by management).
* **Soft Elements**: Shared Values (core superordinate goals), Style (leadership behavior), Staff (talent capabilities), Skills (distinctive organizational competencies).

# 04 — Corporate Portfolio Management

Corporate strategy allocates scarce financial and managerial capital across strategic business units (SBUs) to optimize cash flow, growth, and long-term risk.

### BCG Growth-Share Matrix (2x2)

* **Stars (High Growth, High Relative Market Share)**: Rapid growth engines requiring heavy reinvestment to sustain leadership; mature into Cash Cows.
* **Cash Cows (Low Growth, High Relative Market Share)**: Mature cash generators with low capital needs; fund corporate dividends, R&D, and Question Marks.
* **Question Marks (High Growth, Low Relative Market Share)**: High cash consumers with uncertain trajectories; require decisive investment or selective divestment.
* **Dogs (Low Growth, Low Relative Market Share)**: Cash neutral or drain; candidates for rationalization, harvest, or divestment.

# 05 — Blue Ocean Strategy & Value Innovation

Blue Ocean Strategy shifts the managerial mindset from competing in crowded market spaces ("Red Oceans") to creating uncontested market space ("Blue Oceans").

<Callout type="insight" title="The Four Actions Framework (ERRC Grid)">
  * **Eliminate**: Which industry-standard factors taken for granted should be eliminated?
  * **Reduce**: Which factors should be reduced well below industry standards?
  * **Raise**: Which factors should be raised well above industry standards?
  * **Create**: Which factors should be created that the industry has never offered?
</Callout>

### Case Focus: NVIDIA Corporation (INSEAD Case IN2027)

* **Strategic Dilemma**: Facing commodity commoditization in the PC graphics chip market against giants Intel and AMD.
* **Value Innovation**: Shifted from discrete PC hardware acceleration to the CUDA software-hardware parallel computing platform.
* **Noncustomers Captured**: Scientific supercomputing researchers, biomedical modelers, financial quantitative engineers, and generative AI researchers.
* **Imitation Barriers**: Software-hardware co-design network effects; developers wrote exclusively for CUDA libraries, locking in enterprise workloads.

# 06 — Technology for Business Strategy

Technology transforms from an operational cost center to an enterprise strategic differentiator.

### The 5 Vs of Big Data
1. **Volume**: Scale of data generated from distributed sources.
2. **Velocity**: Speed of generation, streaming, and decision ingestion.
3. **Variety**: Structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data types.
4. **Veracity**: Trustworthiness, noise ratio, and governance integrity.
5. **Value**: Conversion of raw data into measurable economic return.

### Analytics Maturity Continuum
$$\text{Reporting (Descriptive)} \longrightarrow \text{Diagnostic (Why)} \longrightarrow \text{Predictive (Forecasting)} \longrightarrow \text{Prescriptive (Automated Action)}$$

# 07 — Sustainability & ESG Governance

Corporate governance must address long-term environmental, social, and stakeholder viability.

### Dunphy, Griffiths & Benn Sustainability Phase Model (3 Waves, 6 Phases)

* **Wave 1 — Oppositional / Non-Compliance**:
  * *Phase 1: Rejection* — Active exploitation and evasion of environmental responsibility.
  * *Phase 2: Non-Responsiveness* — Environmental disregard as business-as-usual until legally compelled.
* **Wave 2 — Compliance & Efficiency**:
  * *Phase 3: Compliance* — Minimum adherence to environmental and labor legislation to avoid fines.
  * *Phase 4: Efficiency* — Proactive waste reduction, energy efficiency, and cost optimization.
* **Wave 3 — Strategic Transformation**:
  * *Phase 5: Strategic Proactivity* — Sustainability integrated as a core competitive differentiator and brand driver.
  * *Phase 6: The Sustaining Corporation* — Enterprise actively regenerates ecosystems and advances societal flourishing.
