# 02 — FOUNDATIONS: The Tripartite Pedagogical Structure

The LSCM curriculum is divided into three pedagogical pillars governed by three specialized faculty leads:

1. **Strategic Architecture & Dynamics (Prof. Ajit Maurya)**:
   * Marshall Fisher’s Strategic Fit Frontier (Functional vs Innovative).
   * Chopra & Meindl’s Six Logistical and Cross-Functional Drivers.
   * The Bullwhip Effect: 4 root causes, variance amplification ratio, and Beer Distribution Game dynamics.
2. **Quantitative Inventory Optimization (Prof. Manoj Dagaonkar)**:
   * Deterministic lot sizing: Classical EOQ derivation, Total Cost curve calculus, and Economic Production Quantity (EPQ).
   * All-units and incremental quantity discount schedules.
   * Stochastic safety stock modeling under uncertain demand and replenishment lead time.

# 06 — CASES & APPLICATIONS: MIT Beer Distribution Game Dynamics

* **Simulation Origin**: Developed at MIT Sloan School of Management by Jay Forrester to illustrate systemic supply chain instability.
* **Network Topology**: A 4-echelon linear supply chain: **Consumer $\to$ Retailer $\to$ Wholesaler $\to$ Distributor $\to$ Factory**.
* **Operational Rules**:
  * Each echelon makes independent ordering decisions without direct communication.
  * 2-week order transmission delay and 2-week physical shipping delay between tiers.
  * Constant initial customer demand of 4 cases/week.
* **The Experimental Shock**:
  * In Week 5, customer demand rises permanently from 4 cases/week to 8 cases/week.
* **Systemic Failure Modes Observed Across Thousands of Sessions**:
  1. *Downstream Panic*: Retailer experiences brief backorders, panics, and doubles orders to 16 cases/week.
  2. *Upstream Amplification*: Wholesaler, observing the surge and seeking to replenish depleted safety stock, orders 28 cases. Distributor orders 40 cases. By the time demand reaches the Factory, orders surge past 60 cases/week.
  3. *The Hangover*: When factory production finally clears the 4-week pipeline lag, all tiers are flooded with hundreds of cases of excess beer while end-consumer demand remains stable at 8 cases/week. Orders collapse to 0 cases across all tiers for weeks.
* **Pedagogical Takeaway**: The Bullwhip Effect is NOT caused by human malice or consumer panic; it is an inevitable mathematical consequence of **delays and lack of shared point-of-sale visibility** in decoupled supply chains.

    <p>The game models a linear 4-echelon supply chain (Retailer, Wholesaler, Distributor, Factory) with 2-week information and physical shipping delays between nodes. In Week 5, customer demand steps up from 4 to 8 cases/week and stays constant.</p>
