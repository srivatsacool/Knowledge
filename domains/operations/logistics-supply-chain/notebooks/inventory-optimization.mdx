# 03 — CORE CONCEPTS: Classical EOQ & The Total Cost Equilibrium

The Ford W. Harris (1913) model identifies the optimal purchase batch size ($Q^*$) that minimizes total annual variable inventory costs by finding the exact intersection between ordering costs and holding costs.

### Mathematical Derivation of Total Cost Curve
The total annual inventory cost function comprises annual purchase cost, ordering cost, and inventory holding cost:

$$
TC(Q) = D \cdot C + \frac{D}{Q} S + \frac{Q}{2} H
$$

To minimize $TC(Q)$ with respect to order quantity $Q$, we take the first derivative and set it equal to zero:

$$
\frac{d TC(Q)}{dQ} = -\frac{D \cdot S}{Q^2} + \frac{H}{2} = 0
$$

Solving for $Q$:

$$
\frac{D \cdot S}{Q^2} = \frac{H}{2} \implies Q^2 = \frac{2 D S}{H} \implies Q^* = \sqrt{\frac{2 D S}{H}}
$$

# 04 — FRAMEWORKS & MODELS: EPQ, Quantity Discounts & Stochastic Safety Stock

The classical EOQ model assumes instantaneous replenishment and deterministic demand. In production environments and global distribution networks, these premises must be extended across three core frameworks:

### 1. Economic Production Quantity (EPQ)
When replenishment occurs gradually at a daily production rate $p$ while consumption continues at daily demand rate $d$ ($p > d$), inventory builds at rate $(p - d)$. The maximum inventory level never reaches batch size $Q$, reducing carrying charges:

$$
Q^*_{EPQ} = \sqrt{\frac{2 D S}{H \left(1 - \frac{d}{p}\right)}}
$$

Because $(1 - d/p) < 1$, the EPQ batch size is strictly larger than the purchase EOQ ($Q^*_{EPQ} > Q^*_{EOQ}$), compensating for continuous production consumption.

### 2. All-Units vs. Incremental Quantity Discount Tiers
Suppliers incentivize larger lot sizes via price schedules. To evaluate an all-units discount:
1. Compute standard $EOQ$ at base price $C_0$.
2. If $EOQ$ falls below the discount threshold $Q_{min}$, calculate total annual cost $TC(Q)$ at $EOQ$ versus $TC(Q_{min})$ using the discounted purchase price $C_{disc}$ and recalculated holding cost $H_{disc} = i \times C_{disc}$.
3. Choose the lot size with the minimum absolute Total Annual Cost.

### 3. Stochastic Demand & Continuous Review Policy $(s, Q)$
Under stochastic market conditions where daily demand $d$ and supplier lead time $L$ fluctuate, maintaining zero safety stock leads to frequent customer delivery stockouts.

    <h4>1. Total Cost Formulation</h4>
    <p>Total annual variable cost $TC(Q) = \text{Annual Ordering Cost} + \text{Annual Carrying Cost}$:</p>
    $$TC(Q) = \frac{D}{Q}S + \frac{Q}{2}H$$

    <h4>2. First Derivative Condition for Minimization</h4>
    <p>Differentiating $TC(Q)$ with respect to $Q$:</p>
    $$\frac{d TC(Q)}{dQ} = -\frac{D \cdot S}{Q^2} + \frac{H}{2} = 0$$
    $$\frac{D \cdot S}{Q^2} = \frac{H}{2} \implies Q^2 = \frac{2DS}{H} \implies Q^* = \sqrt{\frac{2DS}{H}}$$

    <p>Substitute $Q^* = \sqrt{2DS/H}$ into each individual cost component:</p>
    <ul>

      <li>Annual Holding Cost: $\frac{\sqrt{2DS/H}}{2} \cdot H = \sqrt{\frac{2DS \cdot H^2}{4H}} = \sqrt{\frac{D \cdot S \cdot H}{2}}$</li>
    </ul>
    <p>Because both expressions simplify identically to $\sqrt{\frac{DSH}{2}}$, annual ordering cost strictly equals annual carrying cost at the optimum.</p>
